TOCKLAI TEA RESEARCH INSTITUTE — COMPREHENSIVE GUIDELINES FOR ASSAM TEA CULTIVATION
======================================================================================

Published by: Tea Research Association (TRA), Tocklai Tea Research Institute, Jorhat, Assam
Reference Guide for Tea Planters, Garden Managers, and Field Workers

SECTION 1: SOIL MANAGEMENT
---------------------------
Optimal soil pH for Assam tea is between 4.5 and 5.5. Tea plants thrive in acidic soils.
If soil pH drops below 4.5, apply dolomitic limestone at the rate of 2-4 tonnes per hectare.
If soil pH rises above 5.5, apply aluminum sulfate at 500 kg per hectare to restore acidity.
Soil must have deep drainage (minimum 2 meters depth) to prevent waterlogging during monsoons.
Sandy loam to clay loam soils with good organic matter content (minimum 2%) are ideal.
Apply organic matter (compost, green manure) annually at 10-15 tonnes per hectare.
Conduct soil testing every 6 months from Tocklai-certified laboratories.

Recommended Fertilizer Schedule (per hectare per year):
- Nitrogen (N): 120-160 kg, applied in 4 split doses (March, May, July, September)
- Phosphorus (P2O5): 40-60 kg, applied once in February
- Potassium (K2O): 60-80 kg, applied in 2 splits (March and July)
- Sulphur (S): 30-40 kg annually
- Zinc Sulphate: 10-15 kg if deficiency symptoms observed

SECTION 2: TEA PLANT VARIETIES AND CLONES
------------------------------------------
Tocklai Vegetative (TV) Clones recommended for Assam:
- TV-1: High yielding, suitable for CTC manufacture, drought tolerant
- TV-9: Excellent cup quality, suitable for both CTC and Orthodox
- TV-18: High yielding with good drought tolerance, CTC manufacture
- TV-20: Superior quality clone, recommended for Orthodox manufacture
- TV-22: Vigorous growth, high yield, good for CTC
- TV-23: Best quality clone for Orthodox tea, produces golden tips
- TV-25: Suitable for organic cultivation, pest resistant
- TV-26: High yielding, tolerant to blister blight

Assam Jat vs China Jat:
- Assam Jat: Large leaves, strong malty flavor, suited for CTC processing
- China Jat: Smaller leaves, delicate flavor, suited for Orthodox and specialty teas

SECTION 3: PEST AND DISEASE MANAGEMENT
---------------------------------------
Tea Mosquito Bug (Helopeltis theivora):
- Symptoms: Black spots on young leaves, curled and deformed shoots
- Peak season: March-May and September-November
- Treatment: Localized spraying of Thiamethoxam 25% WG at 0.2 g/litre
- Safety: Do not spray within 7 days of plucking. Use targeted spraying, not blanket application.
- Cultural control: Maintain shade at 40-50% canopy cover, remove shade tree prunings

Red Spider Mite (Oligonychus coffeae):
- Symptoms: Leaves turn coppery brown on upper surface, fine webbing underneath
- Peak season: Dry hot periods (February-April)
- Treatment: Spiromesifen 240 SC at 0.8 ml/litre or Fenpyroximate 5% EC at 1 ml/litre
- Prevention: Maintain adequate shade, avoid water stress, encourage predatory mites

Blister Blight (Exobasidium vexans):
- Symptoms: Circular blisters on young leaves, white powdery growth underneath
- Peak season: High humidity periods with frequent rainfall
- Treatment: Copper Oxychloride 50% WP at 3 g/litre or Hexaconazole 5% EC at 1 ml/litre
- Spray interval: Every 7-10 days during infection period
- Prevention: Ensure good air circulation, avoid dense canopy

Looper Caterpillar (Buzura suppressaria):
- Symptoms: Irregular holes in mature leaves, defoliation
- Treatment: Quinalphos 25% EC at 2 ml/litre
- Biological control: Encourage bird population, use pheromone traps

Root Disease (Poria hypolateritia):
- Symptoms: Yellowing and wilting of bush, white mycelial growth on roots
- Treatment: Remove infected bush and surrounding soil. Apply Trichoderma viride at 50g per plant
- Prevention: Avoid waterlogging, maintain proper drainage

SECTION 4: PLUCKING STANDARDS
-------------------------------
Fine Plucking: Two leaves and a bud — highest quality, recommended for all premium teas
Coarse Plucking: Three leaves and a bud — acceptable during peak flush but reduces quality
Plucking Round: 7-10 days during growing season (March-November)
Plucking Table: Maintain at 60-75 cm height for optimal yield and quality
Annual yield target: 2000-3000 kg made tea per hectare for well-maintained gardens

First Flush (March-April): Produces the most aromatic and premium tea
Second Flush (May-June): Famous for muscatel character, highest commercial value
Monsoon Flush (July-September): Highest volume but lower quality
Autumn Flush (October-November): Good body and flavor, moderate quality

SECTION 5: PROCESSING — CTC AND ORTHODOX
------------------------------------------
CTC (Crush, Tear, Curl) Processing:
- Withering: 16-20 hours, moisture reduction to 68-70%
- CTC Cutting: 3-4 passes through CTC rollers
- Fermentation: 60-90 minutes at 25-28°C, humidity above 95%
- Drying: Fluid bed dryer at 100-110°C, final moisture 2-3%
- CTC Grades: BP, BOP, BPS, PF, PD, CD, D

Orthodox Processing:
- Withering: 14-18 hours, moisture reduction to 60-65%
- Rolling: 30-40 minutes in orthodox roller
- Fermentation: 2-4 hours at 22-26°C
- Drying: 20-25 minutes at 85-90°C, final moisture 2-3%
- Orthodox Grades: SFTGFOP, FTGFOP, TGFOP, GFOP, FOP, OP, BOP, FBOP, GOF, FOF, PD, RD

SECTION 6: CLIMATE ADAPTATION
-------------------------------
Annual rainfall requirement: 2000-3000 mm, well-distributed throughout the year
Optimal temperature range: 20-30°C
Critical temperature: Below 13°C causes dormancy, above 35°C causes heat stress
Humidity: 70-80% relative humidity is ideal for tea growth

Shade Tree Management:
- Recommended species: Albizia odoratissima, Albizia lebbeck, Derris robusta
- Canopy cover: Maintain at 40-50% during growing season
- Pruning: Annual lopping in December-January

Frost Protection:
- Vulnerable period: December-February in upper Assam
- Protection: Smoke screens, overhead sprinkling irrigation
- Recovery: Light pruning of frost-damaged sections in March

Drought Management:
- Critical period: February-March before pre-monsoon rains
- Mulching: Apply at 15-20 cm thickness between rows
- Irrigation: Drip irrigation at 25-30 litres per bush per week during drought

SECTION 7: STORAGE AND WAREHOUSING
------------------------------------
Pre-monsoon humidity above 80% in storage warehouses causes critical fungal spoilage risk for premium Orthodox and CTC grades.
Optimal storage conditions: Temperature 20-25°C, Humidity below 65%
Use moisture barriers (polythene-lined plywood chests) for export consignments
Maximum storage duration: 6 months for CTC, 12 months for Orthodox in proper conditions
Pest control in warehouses: Monthly fumigation with Aluminium Phosphide tablets

SECTION 8: TEA GRADING STANDARDS
----------------------------------
Assam CTC Grades (by particle size):
- BP (Broken Pekoe): Largest CTC grade, strong liquor
- BOP (Broken Orange Pekoe): Medium size, brisk and bright
- BPS (Broken Pekoe Souchong): Slightly coarser grade
- PF (Pekoe Fannings): Fine grade, quick brewing
- PD (Pekoe Dust): Very fine, used in tea bags
- CD (Churamoni Dust): Finest CTC grade
- D (Dust): Standard dust grade

Assam Orthodox Grades (by leaf size and tip content):
- SFTGFOP (Super Fine Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe): Highest grade, abundant golden tips
- FTGFOP (Fine Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe): Premium grade with golden tips
- TGFOP (Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe): Standard premium grade
- GFOP (Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe): Good grade with some tips
- FOP (Flowery Orange Pekoe): Standard whole leaf grade
- OP (Orange Pekoe): Basic whole leaf grade
- BOP (Broken Orange Pekoe): Broken leaf grade, brisk liquor
- FBOP (Flowery Broken Orange Pekoe): Broken with tips

SECTION 10: ORGANIC TEA CULTIVATION
-------------------------------------
Organic certification requires 3-year conversion period from conventional cultivation.
Use only approved organic inputs: Neem oil, Karanja oil, Panchagavya, Jeevamrutha
Biological pest control: Chrysoperla carnea for aphids, Trichoderma for soil pathogens
Organic fertilization: Apply vermicompost at 5 tonnes/hectare and green manure crops
Price premium for certified organic Assam tea: 20-40% above conventional prices
Certification bodies: India Organic (NPOP), USDA Organic, EU Organic, JAS (Japan)
